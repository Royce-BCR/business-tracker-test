What is wrong today

Every part of the tracker works on its own, and most of it was built because it was asked for. The trouble is how the parts connect.

You have to know where to look

The menu is organised by function. To find a purchase order, a supplier's invoice or a quote you have to remember which area holds it, and it is often only reachable from inside one project.

Records do not stand up on their own

A supplier shows as a name on a line, not as a page you can open. From a project there is no way to click through to a supplier, fix a detail and come back.

The handover points are unclear

A quote turns into orders, orders into deliveries, deliveries into invoices. Nothing shows how one line becomes the next, so it feels piecemeal even though each piece is useful.

What already exists and will be reused

Most of the plumbing is already in the tracker, so this is mostly a change of layout and a few missing links, not a new system:

  • Links between records and a Back button that returns you to where you were (the navigation stack).
  • Contacts and companies, with their linked projects, quotes and invoices on the right of a record.
  • Quotes, approvals, purchase orders, invoicing, transactions, cash flow, assets and actions, each already built.
  • The design tokens, tables and side panels adopted in the October design batches.
Principle for the whole rebuild. Nothing is typed twice. Every new screen is a different way of looking at records that already exist, and anything new stored must default safely so existing data, including Harvey, Auro and Guy Nunn, loads unchanged.

A quote is part of a process, while a project or a customer is a whole thing the quote serves. That is why quotes, orders and invoices are lists you work in, and customers, projects and suppliers are pages you visit.

Associations work in both directions

The supplier page is the clearest case. Open a supplier you need to pay and you see the quote requests sent to them, the prices they have given, the orders placed, the invoices still to pay, and every project they are linked to. The same links appear from the project side. Cash flow then reads from the same records, so the money picture follows from the chain and is not typed separately.

Subcontractors. Not needed yet, so not built. The supplier record gets a type (materials or subcontractor) now, and the project timeline can show a dated work item that is not Lynton's own labour. Adding subcontract work later is then a day or two, not a rework.

Its header (name, status and quick actions) stays pinned at the top while you scroll, and every section beneath it can be collapsed or expanded. The activity feed is a view of real records

Try the tabs in the wireframe: each tab shows only its own view. Overview holds the headline strip, what is outstanding and the summary tiles; Activity holds the feed; Money and Materials and deliveries each have their own view. The header and tabs stay fixed and the centre scrolls on its own, so the left and right panels stay put while you read down the feed.

There is no separate “pinned” box. A task, a note, a call, a quote sent, a delivery, an invoice and a payment are each a standalone record that keeps its own identity, and the feed pulls each one through as a card. A card shows the record’s type, date and owner, can be edited or ticked off in place, and links back to the record’s own page (a task in Tasks, an invoice in Invoices, a contact in Contacts). Nothing is copied as text. Edit it in the feed and the source record changes, because they are the same record.

Order is automatic. Open tasks sit at the top of the feed, ordered by due date with the oldest (the most overdue) first, so what is waiting longest is read first. Below them, the history runs newest first. When a task is ticked off it leaves the top and drops into the history on its completion date.

Every field is a field: locked, calculated or editable

The left panel follows one rule. Everything is shown as a field, and the field tells you what you can do with it. A locked field (the reference) is greyed and never changes. A calculated field (value, the two margins, agreed date, hours planned and logged) is greyed too, with a line saying where to change it, for example “Edit the value in Quote Q-101-2”. Margin shows as two boxes, Sold and Now. Editable fields (job type, owner, source, start, complete, duration) look editable and a changed one is highlighted.

Start, complete and duration are linked: change one and the others follow, counting working days. As soon as a date changes, a bar appears at the foot of the panel with three choices. Confirm and update project opens the Plan tab with every linked delivery, invoice and milestone shifted for you to adjust, then confirm. Tentative change opens the same view but keeps the dates flagged as tentative, and warns if they overlap another project so you know who to speak to first. Cancel puts everything back. Other edits (owner, job type, source) just ask Save update or Cancel. Try it: change the start date in the wireframe.

Money and Materials and deliveries are working views

Only the Overview carries the pinned dashboard strip. Every other tab has its own header. Activity starts with a one-line strip of the nine key milestones (done, next, to come), then the feed. Money starts with four numbers and a simple graph of money in and money out, planned (dashed) against actual (solid), with a marker for today. Materials and deliveries starts with a planned-against-actual date graph for each order, with late deliveries drawn in red.

Beneath each graph is the same sequential list, in date order, of what is planned and what has happened: invoices and payments in Money, orders and deliveries in Materials. Each planned line has one button (Mark received, Raise invoice, Confirm paid, Confirm ordered, Confirm delivered, Confirm prices). Pressing it confirms the item on the spot, it stops being planned and becomes actual, and the graph and the milestones update. In the wireframe only the row changes when you press a button; in the build the graph and milestones follow. The list is date-bound and sequential like the timeline, but it is a to-do list of confirmations as much as a history.

The ordering chain, from site visit to payment

Most jobs have one up-front order split across several suppliers (fixings from Screwfix, timber from the local merchant), with room for a second order or delivery. Today most suppliers are paid up front, because there are no trade accounts yet.

  1. ScopeSite visit and listItems and labour listed from the visit. Gerry reviews materials and labour before the quote goes out.
  2. Quote requestsSeveral prices per lineEach line can hold prices from more than one supplier, so suppliers compete.
  3. CompareWhole basketsThe decision view shows total landed cost per supplier, delivery included. Cheapest is flagged. Overrides are recorded with a reason.
  4. OrdersOne per supplierChoosing a supplier creates the order, with its delivery date. A second order or delivery can be added later.
  5. DeliveriesSign off on siteA checklist of the order lines, ticked as they come off the van. Short quantities, substitutions and damage are flagged, with a photo option.
  6. Invoice checkConfirm final pricesAttach the email invoice, photo or receipt, pick the order, confirm actual prices. Differences are recorded as variances.
  7. PayMoney movesPayment is recorded against the order and feeds transactions and cash flow. With a trade account the same chain applies, with a gap before paying.

Rules agreed for the chain

  • Marking a delivery complete asks: check final prices now, or later? Later is expected about nine times in ten and becomes a "confirm prices" task on Today and on the order until done. A short or substituted delivery is marked "price to confirm" automatically.
  • Small differences are logged with a quick reason and carry on. Anything over a threshold needs a short note and appears for review. The threshold is a setting and defaults to 0 percent, so every variance is flagged and given a reason. Raise it later if that proves too noisy.
  • Hire is an option on a line. Any quote or order line can be marked as hire instead of buy, with the hire supplier and cost. It counts as a supplier cost on the job like any other line. Whether to buy or hire a bigger tool is still weighed in the asset plan.
  • Logging a receipt takes under a minute, by whoever holds it, from a phone on site if needed.

Tasks

One task engine and two homes. Jobs and customers are one mode of work, and working on the business is another. Mixing them is how admin gets buried.

Tasks hub, for jobs and customers

  • Chase a quote, confirm prices, sign off a delivery, ring the council about permissions.
  • Attached to a project, customer, quote, order or supplier, and shown in the centre of that record.
  • Filter by person, customer, project, due date and type. Overdue in red.
  • Created automatically by the system (follow-ups, price checks) or by hand at any time.

Business area, for working on the business

  • VAT return, insurance renewal, setting up a trade account.
  • Attached to nobody. Visible to Lynton and Royce to start with, with Mike's access decided when he has a login.
  • Today shows it as its own tile, so a VAT deadline cannot hide.

One task form for both

Title, description, due date, priority, type, assignee, status, recurrence and a reminder time. Job tasks link to their record automatically. Recurring tasks, such as the quarterly VAT return, repeat on their own.

Five working stages and one end stage. A job enters at Planning the moment it is won in the Sales Pipeline, with the contract signed and the deposit invoice raised, so there is no separate stage for the signed contract. Each stage lines up with the nine milestone dates the tracker already holds, so existing projects slot in without reshaping any data. Stage payments, such as a deposit or a mid-job instalment, follow the quote's payment schedule and appear on the project's timeline. They do not need stages of their own.

Lead stage

  • Enquiry capture. Enquiries arrive by email, WhatsApp, phone or in person, and all land in one place as a new lead with contact details, a note on the first conversation and a location if known.
  • Qualification gates move to Settings. Questions, weighting and whether each is a hard gate or a guide are editable. The answers produce a simple score ("strong", "unclear", "weak") shown as a badge on the lead card and on the project page. Default: a hard gate, so a lead cannot be promoted past the gate until its questions are answered. Each question can be switched to a guide in Settings, and the default itself is a setting too.
  • Job type. Some types, such as maintenance, can be marked "not a fit" so they are flagged early.
  • Value and time on every lead. A ballpark value, an estimated duration in days (split into phases if needed), the customer's preferred start and any deadline.
  • Needs attention. The lead board marks any lead that has gone quiet or is missing key information.

Sales stage

  • Follow-up. When a quote is sent the system suggests a follow-up task, by default three working days out (two to three is the range, editable). Lynton chases however he likes, taps "Chased", picks the channel and adds an optional note. The next follow-up is suggested automatically. The gap and the task name are settings.
  • No contact flag. A quote shows "X days since contact" once past a threshold, starting at five working days.
  • Gerry's review. The existing approval process is switched on, so every new quote's materials and labour get one approve or "check this" decision, with a comment, before it goes to the customer.

End stages and the archive

Each board has clear end stages: disqualified for leads, closed lost for sales, completed for projects. Closed lost needs a reason, and if nobody knows why, the reason is "stalled, no response". A record that reaches an end stage leaves the live board and shows in a greyed archive view. Reopening puts it back at the stage it left, with a timeline note of who revived it and when. Nothing is deleted.

Work phases, budgeted hours and the true cost of a deal

The pipeline stages and the work plan are two different things. Mixing them on one board clutters it, so they are split.

Commercial stages

Short, around five or six per board. These are what you drag cards through, and they describe where the deal stands.

The phase plan

Planned tasks with dates, hours and cost: site visit, scope, quote, order, delivery, install phases, sign-off. A template in Settings pre-fills each new job, and each phase can carry its own delivery dates.

Budget by default, confirm by tapping

  1. Each phase template has a standard budget, for example three hours for a site visit including travel.
  2. When a phase comes due it appears on Today. Lynton taps Done, and the system asks "Was that 3 hours?". Yes keeps the budget. Otherwise a scroll picker adjusts it in 15-minute steps.
  3. If he skips the second step, the budget stands and the record is marked "budget, not confirmed".

This is the same tick-then-confirm habit as the delivery sign-off, so there is one pattern to learn across the system.

What it gives you

  • Planned hours times the labour rate gives the budgeted cost. Confirmed hours give the actual. The variance shows per phase and per job, with under-budget shown as a positive.
  • Selling time is costed too, including on deals you never win. You see total selling cost against total won value and what a typical lead costs to qualify, which tells you whether to charge for site visits or qualify harder earlier.
  • Reports show the share of confirmed against assumed hours, so a quietly accepted default cannot make the variance look flat.
  • Quote lines tie to phases, so a material can take the delivery date of the phase it is for. That covers the barbecue shelter example: foundation materials one Monday, structure materials the next.

Calendar and capacity

A cross-project view with layers you switch on and off. It answers what is happening on which day and whether Lynton is free.

Layers

  • Confirmed projects
  • Sales opportunities, weighted by likelihood
  • Leads, weighted
  • Deliveries
  • Business tasks, if wanted

Switch on projects and leads together to see overlaps and gaps, and which leads to prioritise or push back. Clicking a day opens that day on Today.

Capacity

  • Capacity is a setting: the people and their available hours or days each week. Today it is Lynton alone, and Gerry, Mike and Peter can be added.
  • The same capacity view works at all three levels. At the lead stage it shows weighted lead estimates against what is free. At the sales stage it shows opportunities, still weighted. At the project stage it is the detailed phased plan.
  • Every project page has the Gantt, as the phase timeline and that project’s slice of the capacity view. It is a permanent part of the page, not something to drop later.

The weekly review is the forecast, checked

A cash flow forecast is dated money in and out, projected forward. The review adds a check on top. Each expected item, such as an invoice you will raise on Friday when a stage completes and the payment you expect seven days later, gets a status of landed, late or moved. Anything late carries forward with a chase task. This builds on the planned-against-actual dates already in the tracker.

What to look at, and how often

Weekly, fifteen minutes

  • Cash in the bank today, and the lowest point in the next six to eight weeks.
  • Money owed to you, and how late it is.
  • Money you owe, and what falls due before the next customer payment.
  • Expected against actual for the week.
  • Quotes out and follow-ups due, because that is next month's cash.

Monthly, an hour

  • Gross margin per job, quoted against actual.
  • Business costs against plan, and the break-even figure.
  • Profit after set-asides, then the profit share.
  • Pipeline weighted by likelihood, against break-even.
  • Hours confirmed against budget, and selling time spent on lost deals.

Quarterly, a sit-down

  • Cash runway at current costs with no new sales.
  • Which job types make money per day of Lynton's time.
  • Quote win rate, and time from enquiry to decision.
  • Customer concentration.
  • VAT and tax reserve, and the asset pot against the register.
  • A rolling three to six month forecast, refreshed.
If you only watch three numbers: the lowest cash point, overdue money, and margin per job.

Business costs, assets and set-asides

Costs fall into two kinds. Keeping them apart is what makes the profit figures honest.

Job costs

Exist because of a specific project: materials, delivery, hire, subcontractors, and an optional tool-use charge. They count against the job's margin.

Business costs

Paid whether or not a job is running: insurance, subscriptions (website, AI tools), accountancy, phone, and most fuel unless mileage is charged to jobs. They are covered by the margin from all jobs together.

Business costs page

Each cost has an amount, how often it recurs, when it is next due and a category. Recurring ones feed the cash flow forecast, and a matching transaction ticks them off as paid. At the moment overheads are five yearly figures in the Pricing Model (insurance, accounting, vehicle, tools, marketing), spread evenly through the year. The page replaces those with real items and dates, so the monthly overhead figure follows from the list. It also gives the break-even: monthly business costs divided by average gross margin percentage is the sales needed each month.

The build still reads the current calculation first and reports before changing anything, to make sure the code matches this.

Fixed or percentage

A fixed set-aside puts aside, say, £200 a month whatever happens, which suits things that must be funded, like replacing kit. A percentage puts aside a share of whatever profit the month makes, which suits things that should flex, like tax. Each bucket gets a method in Settings. The default stays percentage, so nothing changes until you switch one.

Assets, in plain terms

  • Register. Anything above a set value goes on the register with its cost and date. Cheaper kit is just a running cost. The tracker's register already holds cost, purchase date, useful life (five years by default), residual value, service and insurance dates.
  • Replacement pot. Cost less residual, divided by life, is the yearly wear and so what to put aside to replace it. That comes from the Assets set-aside each month, not from each job.
  • Optional usage charge. A job that needs a tool can carry a charge, which helps quote competitively and recovers the cost over time. Once recovered, the charge can stop or carry on at a small amount. It is off by default, per asset.
  • Buy or hire. A plan item can hold a hire cost for comparison. For a tile cutter needed on three jobs, the plan shows when buying beats hiring. The asset plan already tracks priority, status and funding for wishlist items.

Reports, and where the numbers come from

Finance pages are live working views. Reports are the questions you ask on a schedule, each with a date range. Both read the same records, and the people keeping those records up to date are the only input.

Job reports

  • Quoted against actual per job
  • Variance by cause
  • Hours budgeted against confirmed
  • Selling cost per deal, won and lost
  • Delivery and supplier performance

Business reports

  • Profit and loss
  • Cash runway
  • Win rate, and margin by job type
  • Customer concentration and overdue ageing
  • A monthly board-style summary

The rules that keep the numbers reliable

  1. Every transaction has a type (job cost or business cost) and, for job costs, a project. An uncategorised one shows as a task on Today.
  2. Every money event links to what it settles, an invoice code or an order reference, so expected items tick off by themselves. The tracker already carries a linked code on transactions.
  3. One date per fact: invoiced, paid, delivered, confirmed. A planned date never counts as done.
  4. Reconcile monthly. A reconciled month locks its actuals, and the tracker already does this.
  5. Nothing is typed twice. A wrong figure in a report is fixed by correcting the record behind it, and every figure opens its lines to show which.
  6. A data-quality strip on every report says how far to trust it: how many transactions are uncategorised, invoices missing dates, or hours still assumed.
What is missing today. A job cost or business cost flag on transaction types (the current types include Overhead and Equipment, but nothing maps them), supplier invoices with confirmed prices, customer type and job type, selling-time hours, and a list of business costs. Stages 2, 3, 4 and 5 add these. Stage 2 and the stages after it capture the data in the right shape from the start, and stage 5 tidies what is already there.

Build plan

Seven stages after a prerequisites step, down from eleven. Each stage groups work that depends on the same foundations, and ends with something you can see and check in a few minutes. Every stage is safe to stop after, and inside each stage the steps are in a fixed order with a short summary after each one.

Rules for every stage. Build on the test site only (test repository, Supabase row 2). Never write to the live row. Stop and ask before changing login, the Supabase config, the environment banner, or reshaping stored data. New fields default safely. Reuse the existing design tokens, tables, side panels and Back navigation. Test at tablet and phone width. Finish each step with a plain-English summary, and each stage with the checks below.

Why seven, and in this order

  • The menu and the records first. Navigation was the main frustration, so stage 1 starts by regrouping the menu around the pages that already exist (nothing is removed). Everything else is a view of, or a link between, records, so the record page, the tasks and Today follow. Tasks are merged in because the activity feed is built from them.
  • Buy, then sell, then plan. Ordering was the first pain point, so it comes next. Selling creates the lead durations and values that capacity needs, so planning and capacity come after it.
  • Money in two parts. The foundations (costs, categories, set-asides, assets) come before the pages and reports that read them, and the reports come last because they hold no numbers of their own.
  • The menu grows with the build. Each stage adds its new pages to the right group as they arrive, so the team learns the structure once. Stage 7 is only a final naming and polish pass.

Decisions

Answered on 29 October. Each one is built in as the default, and where it is a setting, it can be changed later without rebuilding anything.

  1. Where Assets livesOperations, as the equipment register, for now. It can move later. Finance shows the set-aside pot.
  2. Set-aside methodPercentage by default (Assets 30, Tax 25, Buffer 20, Growth 25), kept in Settings. A fixed monthly amount is available per pot.
  3. Order of profitNothing comes out ahead of the retained profit number. The profit share is Lynton’s income, and the rest is retained profit, shared into the pots. The build confirms the existing calculation matches before changing anything.
  4. Tool cost on jobsOff by default. It can be switched on where it is needed.
  5. HireAn option on a line item, counted as a supplier cost. The asset plan still holds the buy-or-hire comparison.
  6. Variance thresholdEditable in Settings, defaulting to 0 percent, so every variance is flagged.
  7. Qualification gatesA hard gate by default, with the mode set in Settings, per question and overall.
  8. Today list lengthEditable, default ten per tile, with a See more link.
  9. Follow-up gapTwo to three working days, default three, editable. The five-working-day no-contact flag stays.
  10. Who sees business tasks and costsLynton and Royce by default, then Mike and Gerry when they have logins. A per-user visibility setting an admin can adjust is planned, but is not needed yet.
  11. Gerry and Mike’s accessAn approver access type, scoped to commercial, finance or both. Neither has a user yet, so no login is created until you approve it.
  12. Customer typesThe customer lifecycle: lead, opportunity, customer, referrer, evangelist. The names and order are editable in Settings.
  13. Gantt and capacityThe Gantt is a permanent part of every project page, and the capacity view is a core part of selling and planning, at the lead stage, the sales stage and the detailed project stage. Built in stage 4, with the Gantt kept from stage 1.
